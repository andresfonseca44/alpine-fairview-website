How much coverage do you actually need?

Option 1
$4,000 to $8,000
What it typically covers
  • The cremation and basic funeral home services
  • Transportation and paperwork, like death certificates
  • An urn and a simple memorial
Why this range fits

Cremation usually costs less than burial, so this range often covers the essentials without over-buying.

Typical use: cremation, an add-on policy if you already have coverage, or leaving a little extra for the kids / grandkids.
Popular
Option 2
$10,000 to $20,000
Why it costs more
  • A casket, burial plot and vault
  • Headstone or grave marker
  • Opening and closing the grave
  • A service, flowers and transportation
Why we recommend this range

Burial has more separate costs, and they add up quickly. This range helps your family cover a full service.

Typical use: burial, a second policy, or leaving money for your beneficiary.
Option 3
$25,000 to $50,000
What this level can do
  • Replace lost income for a time
  • Pay off debts or a mortgage
  • Cover final expenses
  • Leave a gift or legacy for loved ones
Who it’s for

Choose this if others depend on you financially, or you would like to leave something behind.

Typical use: replacing lost income, paying off debts, or leaving a legacy for loved ones.
Keep in mind: higher coverage amounts mean higher monthly costs. The goal isn’t the biggest number. It’s the amount that fits your family’s real need. Many people also add one of these as a second policy on top of coverage they already have.
